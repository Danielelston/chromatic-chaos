# Naming Conventions

- Settlements and other places are typically named explicitly after real colors. The color tends to reflect the nature of the area itself. For example, a dull color for a humble village or a dark color for a shady city.
- People aren’t usually so literal about the color scheme when it comes to naming. Usually it can be a derivative for a color (Herb -> Herbert), a color in a different language (Blanche), or something related to a color (Fox).
- Common versus uncommon names simply differ in how “fancy” the words sound or how uncommon the color itself is (Ruby vs Delarouge). Typically, names for the actual common colors (red, orange, yellow, etc.) are more pedestrian and would have the same connotation as calling someone “buddy” or “guy”.
- Sometimes when a color is on the border of two main colors, it’s a name for both of them (Ex. Cyana is a common name in both Maradosa and Covalt).
- Surnames are different; they are based on a profession or a trait well-known to their lineage. They typically have a color reflected in them in some way. For example, a family of Flav farmers might have the surname Honeymeadow or Buffields.
